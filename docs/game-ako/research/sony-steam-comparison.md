# Steam vs. Sony: Sustainable Growth Under Investor Pressure

_Discussion summary — Sony PlayStation strategic analysis_

## Starting premise

Steam's business model profits from developer and user experience rather than raw volume growth, which produces a healthier, more sustainable growth curve. The question: what should Sony do to move toward this model, given that (unlike Valve) Sony answers to investor pressure for quarterly margin expansion?

## Why the Steam model isn't directly portable

Valve is privately held and has no quarterly earnings pressure — its 30% cut stays stable because ecosystem health _is_ its profit function. Sony is a public-company segment that must report growth every quarter, and console hardware is typically sold near cost, recouped through software/services attach. Sony cannot adopt Valve's incentive structure because it doesn't have Valve's ownership structure. This is the ceiling on the comparison.

## Where Sony actually has room to move

- **Decouple "growth" from "volume."** Investors want predictable, expanding revenue — not necessarily unit growth. PS Plus / services revenue is the closest Sony has to Valve's ecosystem-health-as-profit model: recurring, forecastable, and not dependent on a hit-driven blockbuster every year.
- **Extend marketplace logic to developers.** The March 2026 PC/Steam distribution reversal is Sony already implicitly conceding that gatekeeping first-party titles off PC cost more in foregone revenue than it protected in exclusivity value. The next logical step is developer-facing: better rev-share tiers, faster cert, less friction — Valve's real edge over Sony has always been how little it makes developers fight the platform, not price.

## Disc decline as evidence for the services pivot

Confirmed via search: Sony will end PlayStation physical disc production by January 2028; digital hit 85% of PS4/PS5 software sales in Q4 FY2025, running ~82% in the most recent quarter. This is a completed transition, not a slow drift, and it supports the services/subscription framing.

**Important distinction drawn out in discussion:** disc decline is a _margin_ story (no manufacturing/distribution cost, no used-game leakage), not an _audience-targeting_ story. It applies equally regardless of whether the buyer is a legacy loyalist or a newcomer — so it doesn't, on its own, prove anything about who Sony is designing games for.

## The "hardcore audience" debate

**Initial framing (challenged):** that Sony's first-party titles (God of War Ragnarök, Spider-Man 2, Horizon Forbidden West) are designed for casual/newcomer audiences, sidelining hardcore players.

**Pushback:** these titles are mechanically dense and lore-dependent — core-audience-credible design that happened to also succeed commercially. The real casualty was the live-service slate (Concord, shuttered GaaS projects), which was a separate, largely failed bet layered on top of the core-audience pipeline, not a redesign of it.

**Correction from Arohbi, adopted as the accurate framing:** "hardcore audience" means **platform-identity loyalists** — people whose PlayStation identity was forged in the 1990s–2000s by queuing for exclusives and platform-specific scarcity — not players who prefer difficult or niche games.

**Resulting diagnosis:** platform loyalty has eroded not because Sony pivoted to casuals, but because of **industry-wide production convergence** — cinematic AAA production quality (Unreal Engine 5, mocap pipelines, top narrative talent) is no longer PlayStation-exclusive. What loyalty used to buy — access to a level of production quality unavailable elsewhere — is no longer scarce. The March 2026 PC reversal compounds this as an _identity injury_: it retroactively converts "this exclusive is mine" into "this was mine, briefly."

## Where this connects to the standing thesis

Because production-quality parity is the actual force eroding loyalty (not a deliberate casual pivot), competing on fidelity/cinematic scope can't rebuild it — that axis is now commoditized industry-wide. This reinforces the **constraint-as-differentiation** pillar already in the Sony thesis: stylized, mechanically distinct, software-level design discipline is the one lever that can't be matched simply by giving every studio the same engine and budget. It's differentiation Sony can defend even after losing hardware/production exclusivity.
